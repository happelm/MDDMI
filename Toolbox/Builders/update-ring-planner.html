      How the page calculates
      • Day 0 is Patch Tuesday, the second Tuesday of the month. The ring offers the update after the quality deferral. The page assumes the device scans on that day at the time you set.
      • With a deadline set, the device downloads and installs as soon as the update is offered. After the install a restart is pending.
      • The deadline counts from the scan that found the update. The grace period counts from the pending restart. Windows forces the restart at whichever ends later – even during active hours, with a final notice 15 minutes before.
      • Auto reboot before deadline = Yes: until then Windows tries to restart outside active hours. No: it waits for the forced restart.
      • The extra row "device off" shows a device that found the update but was switched off before it could install, for example during a vacation. The grace period gives the user time after the install.
      • Microsoft's guidance for quality updates: deferral + deadline + grace no more than 7 days; recommended deadline 1 day for quality and 2 days for feature updates, grace 2 days (quality) and 7 days (feature).
      • Deadline range: the Intune documentation lists 2–30 days, but the portal saved 1 in class (Lab 3.3). The Windows Update CSP accepts 0–30. An update ring has one grace period for quality and feature updates. A pause stops a ring for up to 35 days.

      The real times depend on when each device scans and how long the install takes. Use the timeline to plan and explain, not as a promise. Sources: Microsoft Learn – Enforce compliance deadlines, Policies for update compliance, Update ring settings, Policy CSP – Update.
